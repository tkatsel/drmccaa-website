Requirement — single sign-in

The survey data contains sensitive information, so a separate password was sent out once to protect it. The Professional Development Committee’s stated wish is that signing in to the website is enough — no second password, and the same sign-in also lets a member edit their own entry.

This is the strongest argument for real per-person accounts rather than one shared site password.

Nice to have — member profiles

The survey already holds something close to profile information, including contact details for some people. With each person’s explicit approval, that could become a searchable member profile. Not for launch.

Open questions

The three issues that have held the website up, with the options currently on the table.

Security — accessible but protected

OptionWhat it givesWhere it falls short
One shared site password (Wix-style)Simple, cheap, instantly understoodLow security. Cannot identify who is signed in, so it can never satisfy the Professional Development requirement of editing your own networking entry. Leaks permanently once shared.
Approved account list (Google Sites)Per-person identity, revocable, no passwords for us to hold. Matches the test site already built.Ties members to a Google account. Someone must keep the approved list current as people join and leave.
Per-committee passwordsLimits blast radius on the sensitive survey dataMore passwords to circulate and rotate. The committees explicitly asked for fewer passwords, not more.
Hosted site with real accountsEverything the committees asked for, including member profilesHighest cost and the most to maintain. Needs a funding answer first.
Settled — September 2026

The Board confirmed that three requirements are compulsory: the site must be free to run, maintainable by volunteers, and safe to hold under GDPR. Per-person identity, avoiding a second sign-in, committees editing their own pages, and embedding external tools are all open to reconsideration.

That rules out Wix, Wild Apricot and self-hosted WordPress on cost, and Cloudflare Access on both maintenance and its 50-user free limit. What survives is Google Sites with a Google Group access list — free, with no technical volunteer required, and per-person access as a side-effect rather than a cost.

A single shared password is no longer recommended. It became permissible when per-person identity was relaxed, but under a free-only constraint it buys a weaker platform and a secret that leaks over time, to avoid an administrative task a Google Group already does for nothing.

Maintenance — keeping it useful

The risk is not building the site, it is the site being six months stale by spring. Two things reduce that risk directly:

  • Launch with a clean slate and very little on it, then add. Every page in this mock-up is a page someone has to keep current.
  • Prefer linking and embedding over rebuilding — the book club platform, the podcast host, the calendar. Content maintained elsewhere cannot go stale here.

Costs — how it gets funded

Answered by the requirements review: there is no budget, so the site must be free to run. That removes the funding question as a blocker, and with it the donate tab as a necessity. Self-hosting the podcast is out on the same grounds — storage costs money.

Two decisions still blocking

  1. Does Professional Development accept splitting the networking data? A public, name-free aggregate view on the website — counts, themes, a map without names — and the contact directory kept off the site entirely, with its own access control. This is the one decision the design depends on. It trades away the “no second sign-in” request in exchange for far less maintenance and a much smaller GDPR surface.
  2. Is there a break-glass backup for the administrator account? Responsibility sits with the Communications Committee, with a named main administrator and a handover when the role changes. What is missing is cover for an unplanned departure, which a handover by definition does not provide.

Smaller questions, not blocking

  • Is About & committees public or members-only?
  • Does the newsletter archive stand alone, or sit under Resources?
  • Calendar: embedded and subscribable, or a hand-kept yearplan?
  • Job board: member posts, external feed, or just a links page?
