# Website ownership and handover

**Decision, September 2026.** Ultimate responsibility for the website lies with the
**Communications Committee**. One member of that committee acts as **main administrator and
point of contact**, and the role passes on through a proper handover when that person steps
down.

This document records what the role covers, what must exist for a handover to work, and the
checklist to run when the role changes hands. It is operational and should be kept current —
unlike [the hosting and security options](hosting-and-security-options.md), which is a
point-in-time decision document.

---

## 1. The role

The main administrator is accountable for the site continuing to exist and function. They do
not have to do all the work, and under the responsibilities model most content updates come
from the other committees.

**Owns**

- The domain, the hosting account and the access list (the Google Group), including renewals.
- Granting and removing edit access for the other committees.
- Adding and removing members from the access list.
- Being the named point of contact for anything website-related: broken pages, access problems,
  "can we add a page for X", and any request to correct or delete personal data.
- Running the annual review in §5.

**Does not own**

- Keeping individual committees' content current. That stays with each committee, per the
  responsibilities model in the [site map](prototype/README.md).
- Deciding what goes on the site. That is a Communications Committee or Board decision.

## 2. The gap worth closing now

The decision says *main* administrator, which implies a backup. It is worth being explicit
about one, because "proper handover" covers a planned departure and not an unplanned one —
illness, a lost phone with the second factor on it, a sudden resignation, or simply someone
going quiet.

The cheapest form of a backup is a **break-glass account**: a second association-owned Google
account that holds owner rights on the site, the Group and the registrar, whose credentials
live in the committee's password manager and which nobody uses day to day. It costs nothing,
requires no second volunteer to stay active, and turns "we have lost the website" into "someone
opens the password manager."

Recommended, not yet decided. The alternative — a named deputy administrator — also works and
is arguably better, but depends on finding a second willing volunteer.

## 4. Handover checklist

Run this when the role changes hands. **Transfer ownership; do not just add the new person as
an editor** — an editor loses everything if the original owner's account is closed.

**Before the outgoing admin leaves**

- [ ] Transfer ownership of the Google Site to the incoming admin or the association account.
- [ ] Transfer ownership of every Google Form, Sheet and folder in the register, individually.
- [ ] Make the incoming admin an owner of the Google Group, then remove the outgoing one.
- [ ] Transfer the registrar login, and update the domain contact email if it was personal.
- [ ] Update the break-glass credentials in the password manager, and confirm the incoming admin
      can open the vault.
- [ ] Walk the asset register together and correct anything stale.
- [ ] Update the point-of-contact address anywhere it is published — the site, the newsletter
      footer, committee pages.

**After**

- [ ] Incoming admin signs in to every account in the register and confirms access.
- [ ] Remove the outgoing admin's personal access from everything except what they need in any
      other role they hold.
- [ ] Record the date of handover at the bottom of this document.

**If there was no handover** — the person is unreachable — use the break-glass account, then
run the checklist above retrospectively and rotate every credential.

## 5. Annual review

Once a year, in the same meeting each time:

- [ ] Asset register still accurate; no empty rows.
- [ ] Domain renewal date and payment method still valid.
- [ ] Access list reviewed. An alumni allowlist only ever grows unless someone prunes it —
      agree in advance what removes a person, and apply it.
- [ ] Break-glass credentials still work.
- [ ] Each committee confirms its pages are current, or says which should be retired.
- [ ] Any personal data still held has a reason to be held.
